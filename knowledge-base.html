CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE

Disaster Recovery & System Redundancy Plan

At NovaTech Solutions Hub, our Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP) ensures maximum uptime, data integrity, and rapid restoration of services in the event of a critical system failure, cyber incident, or infrastructure outage.

1. Data Backup & Retention Protocols

  • Automated Backups: Incremental system backups are performed every 4 hours, with full encrypted server backups executed every weekend.
  • Off-site Storage: All critical ticket data and operational databases are mirrored to a secure, geographically isolated cloud server.

2. Recovery Objectives (RTO & RPO)

  • Recovery Time Objective (RTO): Maximum of 4 hours to restore critical Tier-1 services (e.g., Network/Connectivity, Admin Portals).
  • Recovery Point Objective (RPO): Maximum of 1 hour of acceptable data loss, mitigated by high-frequency transactional syncing.

3. System Redundancy & Failover

  • High Availability (HA): Core network infrastructure operates on a dual-node cluster. If the primary node fails, the secondary node automatically takes over with zero downtime.
  • Power Redundancy: All physical server racks are supported by Uninterruptible Power Supplies (UPS) and backup diesel generators.

4. Emergency Escalation Workflow

  • Incident Declaration: Any "Critical" priority ticket logged via the IT Service Desk triggers an immediate SMS/Email alert to Senior Technicians (S. Naidoo / L. Dlamini).
  • Crisis Management: If resolution exceeds 2 hours, the issue is escalated to the IT Director for hardware replacement or full DR environment activation.
IT SECURITY & COMPLIANCE

Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)

This policy outlines the rules and guidelines for using computer equipment, networks, and data provided by NovaTech Solutions Hub. All personnel must adhere to these standards to maintain a secure environment.

  • Authorized Access: Employees must only access systems, networks, and data for which they have explicit authorization. Sharing passwords or multi-factor authentication (MFA) tokens is strictly prohibited.
  • Hardware Usage: Company-issued devices are for official business purposes. Users must not attempt to bypass administrative controls, install unapproved personal software, or physically modify the hardware.
  • Internet & Email Security: Internet usage must not compromise network security. Users must exercise caution with email attachments and immediately report suspected phishing attempts to the Cybersecurity department.
  • Data Protection: Confidential company data, client information, and proprietary assets must not be transmitted to personal email accounts or stored on unapproved third-party cloud platforms.
  • Physical Security: Workstations must be locked (Windows Key + L) whenever left unattended. Portable devices (laptops, tablets) must be physically secured when traveling or working remotely.
PROCUREMENT WORKFLOW

Acquisition Procedures & Approval Processes

To maintain infrastructure standardization, security compliance, and budget control, all IT hardware, software licenses, and service acquisitions must follow this formal workflow:

  • 1. Needs Assessment & Request: The requesting employee or department head must submit a formal Change Request Form detailing the business justification, technical requirements, and estimated cost of the asset.
  • 2. IT Technical Review: The IT Service Desk evaluates the request to ensure compatibility with existing NovaTech infrastructure, security standards, and approved vendor lists.
  • 3. Budget & Financial Approval: Requests under standard departmental limits require approval from the Department Manager. High-value infrastructure requests must be escalated to the IT Director and Finance for final budget sign-off.
  • 4. Procurement & Licensing: Once approved, the IT Procurement Officer generates a Purchase Order (PO) to an authorized vendor. All software subscriptions are purchased centrally to prevent "Shadow IT".
  • 5. Provisioning & Deployment: Upon delivery, new hardware is assigned an asset tag, logged in the IT database, configured with our standard security image, and officially deployed to the user.

Network & Connectivity

Why is my Wi-Fi not working?

Steps: Ensure Wi-Fi is enabled on your device. Restart your device. Check if other devices can connect to the same network. If on a laptop, look for a physical Wi-Fi switch.

Stop & Report: Stop troubleshooting and log a ticket if the network name (SSID) is missing, or if multiple users are experiencing the same issue.

Why can't I connect to the internet?

Steps: Check your ethernet cable connections if hardwired. Verify your router/modem is powered on. Restart your browser and clear the cache.

Stop & Report: Stop if you receive specific proxy or DNS error messages, and report the issue to IT support.

Devices & Hardware

What should I do if my computer is running slowly?

Steps: Close unused applications and browser tabs. Perform a system restart. Check your hard drive for available space.

Stop & Report: Log a ticket if the computer crashes, makes unusual hardware noises, or remains slow after a reboot.

What should I do if my printer is not responding?

Steps: Verify the printer is turned on and has paper/toner. Clear any visible paper jams. Cancel stuck documents in the print queue and restart the printer.

Stop & Report: Report the issue if the printer displays an internal hardware error code or if it disappears completely from your network devices list.

Accounts & Access

What should I do if my password is not working?

Steps: Ensure Caps Lock is off. Carefully retype your password. Try logging into a different company portal to verify if the account is locked.

Stop & Report: If you receive a "Account Locked" message or have forgotten the password entirely, submit a ticket for a secure password reset.

Why can't I access a shared network folder?

Steps: Check your internet/VPN connection. Restart your file explorer. Verify the folder path is correct.

Stop & Report: Log a ticket if you get an "Access Denied" error, as your user permissions may need to be modified.

Ticket Management

How do I report a recurring technical problem?

Steps: Download the IT Ticket Management Workbook. Clearly state that this is a recurring issue and reference any previous ticket numbers if available.

Stop & Report: Do not attempt advanced registry or system changes. Let technical support investigate the root cause.

What information should I include in an IT support ticket?

Steps: Include your name, date/time, affected device, a clear description of the issue, specific error messages, impact on your work, and what you have already tried to fix it.

Escalation Policy

Critical incidents should be prioritized and escalated promptly. Tickets should be escalated when the issue exceeds the current support level's capabilities, remains unresolved beyond the applicable target time, or poses a significant business or security risk.

Level 1 — First-Line Support

Basic troubleshooting, password assistance, connectivity checks, and initial ticket logging.

Level 2 — Technical Support

More complex device, network, software, and recurring technical issues.

Level 3 — Specialist/Senior IT

Critical incidents, complex infrastructure problems, and issues requiring specialist investigation.
